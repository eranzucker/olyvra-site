How it counts

Every day in any of the 29 Schengen countries counts, including the day you enter and the day you leave. On each day of your stay, look back over the last 180 days: the total must not exceed 90. Day 91 within any 180-day window is an overstay, which can lead to fines, removal or an entry ban.

Days in Cyprus, Ireland and other countries outside Schengen do not count. Neither do days spent under a residence permit or long-stay (D) visa of a Schengen country. EU, EEA and Swiss citizens are not subject to the rule.

The 29 Schengen countries are Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, Croatia, Czechia, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, France, Germany, Greece, Hungary, Iceland, Italy, Latvia, Liechtenstein, Lithuania, Luxembourg, Malta, the Netherlands, Norway, Poland, Portugal, Romania, Slovakia, Slovenia, Spain, Sweden and Switzerland.

Border authorities, using the Entry/Exit System record, make the final decision on your authorised stay.

Questions

Does the day I arrive count?

Yes. The day you enter and the day you leave both count as full days, even if you land late at night or leave early in the morning.

When do my days come back?

Each day drops out of the count 180 days after it happened, so days free up gradually rather than all at once. The last day the calculator shows takes this into account.

Is this the same method as the EU's own calculator?

Yes: count back 180 days from each day of your stay and keep the total at 90 or under. The European Commission's short-stay calculator is linked in the sources.
